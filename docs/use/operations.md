# Daily operations

## Tickets

A ticket starts simple: **open, in progress, done**, with a category, photos, assignment to a staff member and comments between resident and management. Each site can add its own statuses ("waiting for parts", "waiting for board approval", "cancelled"), name them and order them.

Tickets are **private** (about one flat; visible to that unit's people and management) or **common** (elevator, garden, entrance; visible to the site or block). Residents can add a "+1" to a common ticket instead of opening a duplicate, and management sees the count.

Outside technicians have no login. A ticket can name a vendor as a label and the manager relays information.

## Announcements

One-way: management publishes, residents read, and the manager sees **who has read**. Questions go through a ticket. There are no comments or reactions.

**The sender chooses the channels** for each message: in app, e-mail, SMS (push with the mobile app). Because the site pays the SMS bill, the person pressing publish sees the recipient and SMS counts first.

## No chat

There is no messaging inside the app. Every request is a ticket and every notice an announcement, so everything is tracked.

## Documents

- **Site documents** (management plan, insurance, contracts, minutes, financial reports) in folders, each visible to the board only, owners, everyone or chosen units.
- **A private folder per unit** (lease, deposit receipt) visible to that unit's owner or tenant and the manager.
- **Generated documents are filed automatically**: statements, receipts, closing statements, debt certificates and meeting minutes land in the right folder with the right visibility.

Replacing a file overwrites it; there are no document versions yet.

## Not yet

Scheduled maintenance (equipment registry, service calendar, inspection reminders) comes after tickets; recurring expenses already cover the money side.
